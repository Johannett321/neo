---
title: Neo Cloud
date: 2026-09-14
---

Neo now keeps your work in Neo Cloud, not on this Mac. You sign in once, and every
workspace, note, meeting and recording is there on every Mac you sign in on. Nothing
has to be synced, because there is only one copy.

## Signing in

The first screen asks you to sign in or make an account, with a username and a
password or with a passkey. Neo Cloud is free, and free includes everything.

If you synced before, your work is already there: sign in with the passkey you
synced with. Projects, notes, meetings, transcripts, icons, pictures and recording
audio all came across.

## What changed

- **Recordings are transcribed and summarised in Neo Cloud.** Audio goes up a
  second at a time while you record, so closing the lid when the meeting ends no
  longer stops the recap. If the network drops, Neo holds on to the audio and sends
  it when it can.
- **Changes show up on your other Macs as you make them.** There is no sync button
  and no strip saying how far behind anything is.
- **Settings → Account** shows your plan and storage, lets you add or change a
  password, and lists the Macs that are signed in, so you can sign a lost one out.
- **Settings → Data** exports everything as one JSON file, including meetings,
  canvases and folders.

## What is gone

- **The data folder in `~/.neo` and the Markdown mirror.** Neo reads neither any
  more. Once you have signed in and found your work, the folder is safe to delete.
- **Working without a connection.** Neo needs to reach Neo Cloud, and says so when
  it cannot.
- **Transcription or recap servers on your own Mac.** Neo Cloud makes those calls
  now, so a server on `localhost` cannot be reached. A local engine has to be at an
  address Neo Cloud can get to.

A recording that had not been transcribed or summarised before the move is marked
as failed rather than started again on its own, so nobody's API key is spent on old
meetings at once. Open the meeting and choose **Try again** to run it.

Your settings and profile start from their defaults, since they were only ever kept
on each Mac.
